## Findings

**C2-01 — Shared-feed recipients disappear into aggregate counts (P2 comprehension; resolved and rechecked).** On the original build, selecting `CAL-1` in Campus of 3 halls only showed `shift out → 15 links, 15 instances` and `tariff out → 3 links, 3 instances`; recipient names were missing. On the refreshed Chrome build, `Browse outgoing links (18)` expands to exact source-port → receiver-instance/port rows under natural hall order (`h01`, `h02`, `h03`). Within each hall, receivers follow consistent natural ID order. For example, `CAL-1 shift out → h02/M-1 shift in`. Each row is a button named `Select receiver …`; activating the h02/M-1 row navigated to hall h02, selected that instance and opened its inspector. I reached the disclosure by Tab and expanded/collapsed it with Enter; recipient rows were then keyboard reachable and Enter navigated to the selected receiver. The selected CAL-1 screenshot is not part of the durable matrix, so this disposition is based on direct Chrome AX and visual evidence from the live interaction.

**C2-02 — Wire direction relies on endpoint text (P2 comprehension; resolved and rechecked).** The original Chiller pilot line from `AHU-1 load out` to `CH-3 load in` had no direction marker, and the legend did not explain direction. On the refreshed build, a visible arrowhead ends at `CH-3 load in`; the legend states, `Model connections carry values from outputs to inputs; arrows point to the receiving endpoint.` On the selected Factory M-1 graph, incident connections remain distinct by type and unrelated edges dim; arrowheads are visible at receiving ports despite crossings. The inspector separately states exact source and destination ports. These cues together explain the model dependency direction without requiring a physical-flow interpretation.

**C2-03 — Workspace and plant counts disagree without explaining the scope (P2 wayfinding; resolved and rechecked).** The original Workspace called both Campus of 3 halls and Factory utilities `20 instances`, while opening them reported 65 and 23 instances. On the refreshed Chrome build, the rows say `20 instance definitions`, and the note explains: `Instance definitions are entries in the plant file. One definition can expand into several instances when you open the plant.` Campus and Factory still correctly show 65 and 23 expanded instances when opened. The card and plant view now describe different scopes explicitly, so the count transition is understandable. Checked at the native 1728 × 940 viewport in light and dark themes; the note fits on one line at this width and remains readable.

**C2-04 — Component purpose is absent from the selected-instance context (P2 comprehension; resolved and rechecked).** On the original build, selecting AHU-1 showed only `ahu · Office block streamsim/ahu`, channels and links. On the refreshed build, its inspector says `Air-handling unit · Office block`, places `Kind: ahu · streamsim/ahu` below as secondary metadata, and exposes the declared component narrative under a collapsed `What this component models` disclosure. Expanded, the description explains the chilled-water coil, load factors and when the coil cannot hold setpoint. It is readable in the inspector without editing the plant; Tab/Enter reaches and toggles the disclosure. CH-3 similarly identifies as `Water-cooled chiller · Chiller hall` and its expanded explanation describes capacity, setpoint, part-load efficiency and derived values. The description starts collapsed, preserving space, and wraps into several lines without colliding with channels. The legacy alarm-playback HEAT-1 has no description metadata and falls back to `heater` plus its location with no empty disclosure or broken layout.

## Supporting observations

- Campus’s instance-level inspector keeps channels, Links in, Links out, Faults, Effectors and States in separate labeled groups. The initial shared-feed aggregate hid recipient names; C2-01’s expanded relationship list now provides them and direct navigation.
- Factory at 50% is a useful system overview but has numerous crossing edges; `Fit the plant in view` trades smaller labels for more context. Selecting one node reduces unrelated edge contrast and makes direction markers on incident connections visible. The M-1 inspector directly answers the tested questions: CAL-1 shift → M-1 shift; R-1 pressure → M-1 air; M-1 air → R-1 demand; CH-1 supply → M-1 coolant; and M-1 heat → CH-1 load. The labels expose both source and receiver ports, so local chain comprehension does not depend on an assumed physical pipe or a global left-to-right layout.
- Relationship groups are visible and named in the plant inspector. Workspace definition counts and expanded plant instance counts now carry distinct labels and an explanation; see C2-03.
- Run context remains visible at the report top: mode, seed, duration and verification badge. When a plant has changed since the recording, a banner says the review and replay use the plant recorded with the run. This tested context is understandable and did not produce another finding.
- Before C2-04, the inspector dropped component descriptions and showed the raw kind. It now displays declared titles and optional descriptions from component metadata, while retaining kind/reference as secondary information and a clean fallback when descriptions are absent.
- The legend identifies wire color/pin shape as port types and filled/hollow as connected/not connected. Since C2-02 it also explains that model connections carry values from outputs to inputs and arrows point to the receiving endpoint.

## Disposition and verdict

The updated recipient list resolves C2-01: grouped counts now have an actionable path to exact relationships and selected endpoints. Arrowheads plus the legend’s model-dependency explanation resolve C2-02. The clarified Workspace note resolves C2-03 and remains legible in both themes at the inspected viewport. Existing component titles and descriptions resolve C2-04 while keeping detailed text optional and preserving a fallback for metadata-free legacy kinds. Tested feedback paths are readable in the selected graph and exact in the inspector; the five-area graph still has crossings at overview, but selection and port-level details give a useful route through them. The final comprehension matrix reports zero axe findings, console errors and horizontal overflow in all 36 captures, and representative 1280 / 1920 screenshots in both themes were independently inspected. No material comprehension friction remains in the scoped flows. **UX and comprehension approval: 10/10** for the tested desktop workspace counts, component identification/descriptions, connection discovery and direction, graph-to-inspector navigation, and model feedback paths (fixes `829160a`, `c022d6c`, `ae3f50b`, `12c1f2e`).
